How to

Everything the app does, in one place.

THE GUIDED TOUR

If you would rather be shown than read, the Collection and the Advanced sync page each have a short tour that spotlights one control at a time. It runs by itself the first time you open either page, and once you close it, whether you reach the end or skip it, it does not come back on its own.

To see it again, press NEED HELP?: on the Collection it sits in the bottom-right corner of the sources card, and on Advanced sync at the end of the connect row. Steps for controls you cannot see yet are skipped, so a tour taken before connecting a device is shorter than one taken after.

THE COLLECTION

The Collection shows every known amiibo, grouped by series. It works with one source, or two:

  • Your folder. Pick the folder holding your dump files (.bin). Owned amiibo light up in colour, missing ones stay grey. Files never leave your computer. In Firefox or Safari the folder opens read-only: tracking works the same, the browser just forgets the folder between visits, so rescanning means picking it again.
  • Loose .bin files. Instead of a folder, press .BIN FILE and pick dumps directly, or a single all-in-one file. Handy when one arrived in Downloads and you do not want to build a folder around it just to look at it. This is a read-only source: it fills in your collection and can be sent to the device with SELECT, but downloads and two-way sync need a real folder, because that is where files and the sync record have to be written. Picking files replaces a connected folder rather than adding to it.
  • Your device. Connect over Bluetooth and pick the folder on the device to read. Its contents appear with a bluetooth badge and count as owned, even before they reach your folder. Reading takes a few minutes, you can stop at any time, and while connected the app quietly pings the device so it does not fall asleep.

Each source is a chip in the panel with its own facts: dump files for the folder, amiibo count and synced bar for the device. Your collection's overall progress sits right below the panel, counting both sources together. The counts double as shortcuts: click OWNED or ON DEVICE to filter by them. From the chip you can rescan a source, change its folder, or remove it. Amiibo are recognised by the identity inside each file, so filenames do not matter.

BROWSE, SEARCH, FILTER

  • Search matches names, series and IDs. Press / to jump straight to it.
  • Filters show all, owned, missing, or, once a device is scanned, what is not on the device yet. Each pill carries its count.
  • Sort series by release date, name, or how complete they are.
  • Cards or List. Cards lead with artwork; the list shows your actual filenames.
  • Every series row carries a tally: owned out of total, a check when the series is complete, a bluetooth mark once a device is scanned, ▲/▼ for what still needs to move, and a sparkle for dumps newer than the database. Hover it for the plain-words version.
  • Something looks wrong with your counts? The toolbar's last button holds SCAN REPORT, which lists every file the scans could not identify, and EXPORT SCAN LOG, which saves everything both scans saw as a small file you can attach to a bug report. It contains filenames, sizes and IDs, never your files themselves.
  • Open series stay open and your scroll position survives. Clicking an amiibo opens its detail page; move through the series with ← and →.

SYNCING

With both sources connected, a SYNC button appears. It fills the gaps in both directions: it downloads what only the device has and sends what only your folder has. It deletes nothing, and both sides end up identical. You review a summary of counts, size, time and device space before anything is written. The panel always shows how far apart the two sides are; the first time they match you get a proper old-game victory screen, and the panel keeps a standing ALL SYNCED line after it.

To move specific amiibo instead, press SELECT, tap the ones you want, then SEND TO DEVICE or DOWNLOAD from the bar at the bottom. Only amiibo missing on the other side are transferred.

In Advanced mode the same bar offers DELETE FROM DEVICE, which removes the selected amiibo from the device and leaves your folder alone. It counts files, not amiibo, and tells you when those two numbers differ. Some amiibo stand for more than one dump: every Happy Home Designer card shares a single identity, and each Air Riders character covers four vehicles. So picking one card really does mean deleting all of them, and the confirmation says so first.

HOW YOUR FOLDER IS LAID OUT

Sync does not care how you file your dumps. An amiibo is identified by the ID inside the file, at byte 84, never by its name or folder, so you can arrange your library however you like and rename anything at any time. Two copies of one collection can share zero paths and still match. MATCH exists for exactly that case.

Folder names only ever matter in one direction: a plain SYNC mirrors your layout onto the device, so what you choose locally is what you get there.

The one case where the app has to invent a name is an amiibo arriving with no file of its own, which happens when it comes out of an all-in-one file. Those are filed as Series/Name.bin:

E:/amiibo/
  Super Smash Bros/Mario.bin
  Animal Crossing/[AC] 001 - Isabelle.bin
  Kirby Air Riders/Kirby.bin
  MSS/Pink Gold Peach - Horse Racing.bin

That last line shows the only wrinkle. The device caps a whole path at 63 bytes and a filename at 47, and it truncates silently rather than complaining, so names are shortened in steps until they fit: the full series name first, then its initials (Mario Sports Superstars becomes MSS), then a shortened amiibo name (Pink Gold Peach - Horse Racing becomes Pink Gold Peach - HR). How much shortening is needed depends on how deep your device folder is. At E:/amiibo only nine of the 946 known amiibo need any at all.

Two amiibo are never given the same filename. Where the database lists the same display name twice, the difference is spelled out rather than counted: Luke (Card) against Luke, Palico v2 against Palico, and for two printings of one Street Fighter 6 card that differ in nothing else, the model number, as in Terry 04e8. The 91 Happy Home Designer cards all carry one ID, so they are filed by tag UID instead, as AC/HHD 04ab17fc2e4080.bin.

A loose .bin you pick on its own is treated differently again: it already has a name you chose, so it keeps it and only gains a series folder. K+WarpStar.bin becomes Kirby Air Riders/K+WarpStar.bin. Only a name too long for the device is replaced by a built one.

Kirby Air Riders dumps need one extra thing. All four vehicles of a character share a single amiibo ID, so a name built from the database would put four real dumps on one path and keep the last. When the app has to name one, it adds the vehicle: KAR/Kirby (Warp).bin. The short form is what fits, since the full name pushes the longest Air Riders path to 72 bytes, nine over the limit.

Nothing here renames files inside a folder you connected. That layout is left exactly as it is.

ALL-IN-ONE FILES

Some tools hand out a whole amiibo library as a single .bin, a few hundred kilobytes holding hundreds of dumps end to end. Drop one in your folder and it is recognised on the next scan, unpacked in memory, and its amiibo are transferred individually. The container itself is never sent: the device cannot read it, and pushing half a megabyte over a 2 kB/s link would cost about four and a half minutes for nothing.

Only what you are missing goes across. A dump inside the file is skipped when you already hold that amiibo in your folder, when the device already has it, and when another all-in-one file in the same folder already offered it. Duplicates inside one file collapse too, whether they are byte-identical or two different tags of the same character. The scan tells you the split, and nothing to transfer is the correct answer for a library you already have in full.

Matching against the device by amiibo rather than by filename needs MATCH, which reads every file on the device. A plain SYNC compares paths only, so it can miss a copy filed under another name. The scan says which of the two it did.

Going the other way, advanced mode adds PACK FOLDER and PACK DEVICE, which write your library out as one all-in-one file you can save or pass on. Files written here are meant to be readable by the other apps that use this format, so each dump is tidied into a well-formed standard tag on the way in.

One thing these files cannot carry. Each slot holds 540 bytes, which is a standard tag. Kirby Air Riders amiibo are larger, and the vehicle is stored past the end of a slot, so all four vehicle pairings for a character collapse into one entry with no vehicle at all. If you already have proper Air Riders dumps, keep them: they are strictly better than any all-in-one copy, and that is why yours are never replaced by one.

.fca files are read too. FCA is a second all-in-one format, and a better one: it stores each dump whole, so Kirby Air Riders vehicles do survive in it. Pick or drop one exactly like a .bin and its amiibo are unpacked the same way, vehicles and all. Anything in the archive that is not an amiibo, such as Skylanders or Lego Dimensions dumps, is listed and left alone. Writing FCA is not supported; the two PACK operations always produce the .bin format.

VEHICLES & CARD SETS

Two collections inside the collection get special treatment:

  • Air Riders vehicles. Every Kirby Air Riders character can be paired with four machines, and each pairing is its own dump. The collection shows a small tally on the card; the detail page shows the machines themselves, lit up once you hold that pairing.
  • Fan-made cards. The 91 Happy Home Designer item cards are a community-made pack, not official Nintendo cards. They all share one ID, so they appear as a single entry; its detail page lists every card and what it unlocks. The set is shown by default; if you only track official amiibo, switch it off in Settings and your totals ignore it. Syncing still copies the files either way.

ADVANCED MODE

Settings → ADVANCED reveals the expert layer:

  • Advanced sync appears in the navigation, with every operation and option: BACKUP copies the whole device, and can re-download files or include system files. SYNC runs two-way with optional deletions and slow verification. MATCH fills gaps by amiibo and ignores folder layout. REPLACE wipes the device and writes your folder fresh, and asks twice before doing so. CHECK reads everything and reports differences without changing a thing.
  • ORGANISE DEVICE and ORGANISE FOLDER tidy one side without touching the other: every dump gets the name and folder the app would have chosen for it in the first place. Nothing is copied and nothing is deleted. Files are moved, and only folders the move leaves empty get cleared away. This is also the cure for a device that has become slow to read, since a few hundred files in one folder is what causes that. Anything it cannot recognise is listed and left exactly where it is, never guessed at.
  • WIPE empties the device folder and writes nothing back. That is the difference from REPLACE, which also clears the device but only on the way to writing your folder over the top. WIPE will not delete the files your device itself needs, and it leaves alone any folder it could not read. It tells you about both before you confirm.
  • Extra detail appears here and there: raw plans, run logs, and format trivia on detail pages. On the Collection, SELECT gains DELETE FROM DEVICE.

WHEN A FOLDER WON’T LIST

Older devices can choke on a folder holding several hundred files. Asking for the list takes longer than the app is willing to wait, so a scan stops before it starts. If that happens, nothing is broken and nothing is lost. Your files are still there.

Two things fix it. ORGANISE DEVICE (Advanced sync) spreads a big pile into one folder per series, which is the permanent cure: the listing cost is paid on every single scan. If the folder has got bad enough that it won’t list at all any more, Settings → DEBUG TOOLS → REPAIR gets the files out first.

What REPAIR does. It reads as far as your device manages, moves those files into a folder called E:/r_, then asks again. Each go reaches further, because the folder is shorter than it was. Nothing is deleted and nothing leaves the device, so you don’t need to pick a folder on your computer first.

Why the rescued files are called 0001.bin. An amiibo is identified by what is inside the file, never by its name, so the original names carry nothing the app needs. Short made-up ones also avoid every problem that long or awkward names cause in the middle of a rescue. Run ORGANISE DEVICE afterwards and they get their real names and folders. Sync and backup ignore E:/r_ until then, so those placeholder names never spread anywhere.

Whenever you connect with files still parked in E:/r_, the app offers to organise them, back them up, or delete them.

What can’t be recovered. If your device can’t read a single entry from the folder, there is nothing any app can do: the only way to open a file is to know its name, and the only way to learn a name is the list that isn’t working. REPAIR will say so plainly rather than pretend otherwise, and erasing the folder at that point destroys files nobody has seen.

TROUBLESHOOTING

“Web Bluetooth unavailable”. Use Chrome or Edge. Safari and Firefox have not implemented it, so the Collection still works with your folder but no device can be connected.

The device won’t connect. It accepts exactly one connection at a time, so close any other tab or app holding it, including the official web tools. Failing that, turn the device off and on.

It connected, then dropped. Devices power themselves down when the link goes quiet. The app pings yours every ten seconds to prevent that, but a long run out of range will still drop. Reconnect and start again: a transfer that was interrupted is resumable, and anything already copied stays copied.

A scan sticks or fails on one folder. Usually too many files in one place, and fixable. See when a folder won’t list.

“Some device folders could not be listed”. Reading them failed twice, so nothing inside them was counted and the plan you are looking at is incomplete. Sync will not delete anything in them either: clearing a folder on the device takes its contents with it, and nobody has seen what is in there. A slow connection is the usual cause, so scan again to retry.

Everything shows as an upload after picking a folder. Your folder and the device folder are probably a level apart. Say your folder contains an amiibo folder while the device is already pointed at E:/amiibo: every path is then shifted by one, and nothing lines up. Point one of them at the other level. The plan warns you when the two sides have nothing in common.

Transfers are slow. They are: this is Bluetooth to a very small device, at roughly a second per amiibo, and slower as the drive fills. A full library is tens of minutes. Long runs can be stopped and picked up later, and the app only ever transfers what differs.

A run stopped part way. Open the RUN LOG on the Advanced sync page and press SAVE JSON. Every operation is in there with its timing and outcome, including exactly what the device refused and why. That file is the thing to share when asking for help.

Something is wrong and you want to look properly. Settings → DEBUG TOOLS, below.

DEBUG TOOLS

Settings → DEBUG TOOLS opens the device internals page. It talks in the device’s own terms rather than in amiibo, which makes it the right place to look when something misbehaves, and safe to ignore entirely otherwise. Each tool connects on its own, one at a time.

  • PROBE is strictly read-only: it creates nothing, changes nothing and deletes nothing. It reports the firmware version, how full the drive is, and a full listing of every folder and file, then optionally reads one file back to check the reported sizes are honest. It ends with a JSON report you can copy or save. If you are reporting a problem, that report is the single most useful thing to send.
  • WRITE TEST checks that writing actually works, without risking anything you own. Everything it does happens inside a scratch folder called E:/_synctest; it refuses to run at all if that folder already has something in it, and it clears up after itself even when a check fails. It measures real transfer speed while it is there, which is where the timing estimates elsewhere come from.
  • REPAIR is the only tool here that changes your device. It is for a folder that will not finish listing: it moves out what it can still read, in batches, so the folder gets short enough to read again. Nothing is deleted by that and nothing leaves the device. Erasing the folder afterwards is a separate step that asks first, and it says plainly whether anything was actually rescued. That matters, because if nothing could be read, erasing destroys files nobody has seen. Full story above.

SETTINGS & TIPS

  • Theme offers three NES palettes, and Pirate twelve coats for the mascot. The COLLECTION toggle shows the fan-made card set. It is on by default; turn it off for official-only totals. Every choice you make here, and the view, sort and filter settings on the Collection, is saved and persists across refreshes and sessions.
  • Syncing needs Chrome or Edge: Bluetooth and writable folder access are not available in other browsers yet. Firefox and Safari can still browse the database and scan a folder read-only to track a collection.
  • Nothing is uploaded anywhere and there is no server. Your folder, your device, your browser.
  • The title screen listens for Enter, like any good title screen.
